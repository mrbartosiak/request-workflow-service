# Production failure modes and recovery

Use the failure modes below to design later verification for the internal request service. The expected behavior in [quality acceptance](quality-acceptance.md) and the workflow invariants still apply. The signals and recovery steps below are candidates. Approve fault scenarios, alerting, recovery targets and operator authority under DEC-14, and any vendor-specific delivery behavior under DEC-09. See [decisions](decisions.md).

## Observed incidents and applicable lessons

- **Database failover with divergent writes.** GitHub reported that a network partition left writes on each side of a failover and made an immediate failback unsafe. It paused webhook delivery while recovering. For this service, exercise verification of acknowledged mutations and reconciliation of pending effects before resuming delivery after a restore or failover. [GitHub October 2018 post-incident analysis](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/oct21-post-incident-analysis/)
- **Routine operations targeting the wrong resources.** Atlassian reported that a maintenance script received cloud site identifiers where app identifiers were intended. The API accepted both, resulting in unintended site deletion and difficult restoration. For this service, verify that wrong target types or scopes are detected before mutation and that restoration preserves business invariants. [Atlassian April 2022 post-incident review](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/how-we-build/post-incident-review-april-2022-outage)

## Reliability guidance

Google SRE guidance describes retries amplifying load on an already saturated service. AWS reliability guidance covers ambiguous effects after a timeout. Exercise overload and ambiguous completion independently, then together, to verify that recovery avoids a second surge and duplicate effects. [Google SRE on cascading failures](https://sre.google/sre-book/addressing-cascading-failures/) · [AWS Builders' Library on timeouts and retries](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/)

## Failure and verification matrix

Linked quality criteria identify where to record evidence. Confirm actual outcomes from the database, delivery records, vendor test records and request measurements as applicable.

| ID | Failure mode and regression signal | Candidate response and recovery evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-01 | Database is unavailable, failover leaves a write uncertain, or an instance stops between commit and response. Signals include a timeout, an unexplained mismatch between acknowledged and durable outcomes, or failed database checks. | Report an unavailable or uncertain outcome in line with DEC-08. Verify persisted state before reapplying uncertain work. In a restart/retry test across instances, compare acknowledged mutations and replay outcomes with durable state. [Q-02](quality-acceptance.md#q-02-database-level-idempotency-across-instances) · [Q-03](quality-acceptance.md#q-03-concurrency-and-persistence-invariants) |
| R-02 | Concurrent commands race on one request or simultaneous publishes contend for IDs. Signals include invalid transition pairs, lost edits, duplicate IDs, or a spike in contention-related failures. | Verify results match a permitted ordering and independent publication IDs remain unique. Record pairwise outcomes and database evidence from conflicting commands across instances. [Q-03](quality-acceptance.md#q-03-concurrency-and-persistence-invariants) |
| R-03 | Traffic surge, slow storage or a dependency causes saturation and synchronized retries. Signals include rising tail latency, pool/wait pressure, timeouts, retry rate or work backlog age. | Limit the amplification of failing work and restore traffic gradually when the dependency recovers. Compare offered and completed rates, error classes and latency with a baseline and report any observed breach of Q-01. Approve fault profiles and recovery targets. [Q-01](quality-acceptance.md#q-01-capacity-and-response-latency) · [Q-10](quality-acceptance.md#q-10-operational-recovery) |
| R-04 | A vendor throttles, returns an ambiguous timeout, or delivers duplicate, late or missing updates. Signals include a backlog, unknown outcomes, unexpected remote counts and stale evidence. | Classify confirmed and uncertain outcomes distinctly. Compare both sides before repeating an ambiguous effect where safe retry is unavailable. Verify the linked request, the external effect count and progress after the vendor recovers. [Q-09](quality-acceptance.md#q-09-reliable-integration-delivery) |
| R-05 | A vendor contract, authorization scope, credential or certificate changes. Signals include mapping failures, rejected callbacks, authentication errors, repeated permanent failures or a dependency that stops sending expected updates. | Make failure visible by operating condition. Validate a proposed configuration or contract change against approved examples, then verify safe continuation or replay of retained work. Record whether the impact is isolated to the affected use case or vendor. [Q-08](quality-acceptance.md#q-08-changeability-and-integration-ease) · [Q-09](quality-acceptance.md#q-09-reliable-integration-delivery) |
| R-06 | Malformed or unrepresentable request data reaches persistence, or a Delete or Reject command succeeds without its mandatory reason. Signals include writes attempted for invalid inputs, bad stored data, or legitimate text being rejected. | Exercise representative invalid and multilingual inputs at the REST boundary. Verify that missing reasons fail validation and invalid input attempts no write. Verify round trips for approved retained and readable fields, including reasons where the applicable policies permit retention and access. [Q-04](quality-acceptance.md#q-04-early-request-validation) |
| R-07 | Request or error logs become unavailable or overrun storage, or expose confidential data. Signals include volume gaps, delayed ingestion, invalid records or sensitive fields in log inspections. | Make loss or delay observable and apply the approved log-sink outage behavior. Exercise sink outage and redaction checks with synthetic sensitive data. Record request impact, diagnostic visibility and any delivery gap. [Q-05](quality-acceptance.md#q-05-request-and-error-logging) |
| R-08 | A misrouted action, changed group assignment or overly broad access affects another request scope. Signals include a wrong-target mutation, unauthorized disclosure, or a stale role producing an unexpected approval. | Check supported access rules, target type and scope before acting. Exercise wrong-scope and stale-mapping cases against the approved access and approval model. Record the user-visible outcome and verify durable state and data visibility. DEC-04 and DEC-12 govern access and corporate approval policy. |
| R-09 | A deployment or migration regresses workflow behavior or makes existing persisted data unusable. Signals include contract drift, post-release errors and failures reading previously valid records. | Test applicable compatibility across rollout or rollback and exercise representative existing records through normal use cases. Record outcomes after migration and recovery, including effects on identifiers and approved vendor reconciliation. [Q-07](quality-acceptance.md#q-07-cyclomatic-complexity-and-testing) · [Q-10](quality-acceptance.md#q-10-operational-recovery) |
| R-10 | Backup is unavailable or incomplete, or restoration leaves local state out of sync with effects already applied externally. Signals include restore failure, missing acknowledged outcomes, missing operation history needed for replay or mismatches with remote records. | Exercise restore in a test environment and check relevant state, authorization, deduplication and delivery invariants before normal service resumes. Record elapsed time, data loss, retained replay knowledge and reconciled external state. Compare with approved recovery objectives. [Q-10](quality-acceptance.md#q-10-operational-recovery) |

## Recovery evidence

For each approved exercise, retain the scenario, application and environment versions, expected outcome, observed user impact, correlated records, durable state comparison and recovery outcome. Where a connector is involved, include its confirmed status and any unreconciled items. Record the time to detect, time to restore, maximum observed data loss and whether replay created extra effects. Apply mandatory outcomes when judging the evidence. Record a human-approved operating exception only for the specific gate and policy that allow it.
